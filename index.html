The emotional arc

How she travels — from stuck to loyal

Follow the temperature. It starts hot with the problem, cools into resignation at the low point, then warms green as relief and trust take over. Creative can enter at any stage — but she has to recognise herself in it.

For creative

She counts in days. Count with her.

Counting shows up in 68 of the overheard quotes. A named day beats an adjective every time: “I haven't been since Thursday” lands harder than “I'm constipated.” It's specific, it's hers, and it makes a scroller check her own count.

For creative

Name the cause, not just the symptom.

Everyone else sells her a fix for constipation. Almost nobody tells her why her gut changed at 45. Naming it does the belief-building for you: she goes from thinking she's failing at something to understanding what happened to her. “Your diet didn't change. Your hormones did.” That's a cold-traffic opener, and it's the one angle in here nobody in the category is running.

Lead with the fact, agitate with the feeling.

Name constipation in the hook (it converts) — then twist the knife with bloated, heavy, never empty. That's the felt layer she recognises in the mirror.

For creative

Name the graveyard out loud.

Listing what failed her — water, fibre, prune juice, laxatives — earns instant belief. It proves you know her, and sets up the killer line: “worked when nothing else did.”

Three details make the recital sharper. She is sick of prunes, not just unconvinced by them, so the fatigue is funnier and truer than the failure. The powders are hated for taste and texture above all — vile, gloopy, gagging — which makes a capsule instead of a gloopy sachet a format wedge we never use. And laxatives cost her the weekend: she plans her Saturday around the loo. That is a sharper enemy than “harsh”.

For creative

Don't fight the scepticism — say it for her.

“I was sceptical at first, but…” is her exact arc. Opening on her doubt (a review-style, contrarian hook) disarms it faster than any claim. “Actually” is your highest-signal word — it means she didn't expect it to work.

Her doubt has a history. Of the overheard quotes that mention a doctor, a run of them are women who went and got nothing back: fobbed off, told it's “just IBS”, and eventually deciding there's no point going again. By the time she reaches your ad she has been dismissed by someone with far more authority than you. Being believed is the thing she's short of, so “you're not imagining it” does more work than any claim about the product.

What it means for creative

The mindset above, distilled into rules you can hold a hook against.

1

Constipation is the entry, bloated is the twist

Name constipation in the hook — the data says it out-converts bloating. Then agitate with the bloated / heavy / never empty feeling she lives in.

2

Meet her scepticism, don't argue with it

"Sceptical at first" is her default, not an objection. Open inside her doubt and let the result surprise her. "Actually" is your tell-tale word.

3

Recite the graveyard of what failed

Water, fibre, prune juice, laxatives, the chemist aisle. Listing them proves you know her — and sets up "worked when nothing else did."

4

Gentle + natural is the wedge against laxatives

Her fear is dependency and cramping. Gentle, natural, no pain, keeps me regular is the exact contrast that separates you from everything she's tried.

5

Sell "normal," never "detox"

She wants regular, light, in control, normal for the first time in years — the quiet return of ordinary. Not a cleanse, not a finish line, a daily habit.

The overheard pull sharpens this into a warning. "Colon cleanses are a scam" is a live opinion in her spaces, and reviews of actual colon-cleanse products skew angry. The category name carries baggage our product doesn't deserve, so lead on gentle and daily and let the name sit quietly behind it.

6

"Wish I'd found it sooner" is a hook, not just a review

The regret of loyal customers speaks straight to the woman still scrolling and suffering. Her happy ending is your best cold-traffic opener.

7

Explain why her gut changed at 45

The biggest gap between what our customers say and what she says before she buys. She knows she's blocked up; nobody has told her why her body stopped working the way it used to. Menopause and HRT are the explanation she reaches for and her GP won't confirm. Naming it moves her from "I'm doing something wrong" to "this happened to me", which is where she becomes buyable.
