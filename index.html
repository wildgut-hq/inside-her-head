The emotional arc

How she travels — from stuck to loyal

Follow the temperature. It starts hot with the problem, cools into resignation at the low point, then warms green as relief and trust take over. Creative can enter at any stage — but she has to recognise herself in it.

Stage 01 · The struggle

This has been going on for years

Constipation is the fact of her life — not a bad week, a permanent state. She measures it in days and years, not hours. This is the ground everything else sits on.

I have had chronic constipation for many years & it can be two weeks before I have a proper bowel movement.

After decades of being constipated. You name it I tried it.

I've always had issues with bowel movements for as long as I can remember.

I was in constant pain from being so blocked up.

I struggle to go naturally and have tried all sorts of things to help.

She says it plainly: “backed up.” “Bunged up.” “Blocked up.”

Stage 02 · The felt layer

But what she feels is heavy, bloated, sluggish

Constipation is the diagnosis. Bloated and heavy is the sensation she lives in all day. This is the difference between naming the problem and making her feel understood.

Still feeling bloated, full and sluggish. Hopefully this will get better.

I always went to the loo regularly but never felt "empty".

No more heavy sluggish feelings.

No more discomfort for days on end.

For creative

Lead with the fact, agitate with the feeling.

Name constipation in the hook (it converts) — then twist the knife with bloated, heavy, never empty. That's the felt layer she recognises in the mirror.

Stage 03 · The graveyard

She's already tried everything you're about to suggest

Water. Fibre. Fruit. Laxatives. Prune juice. The whole chemist aisle. Even the hospital. Assume she's ahead of you — and that the ones that "worked" all quietly stopped.

I have tried various pills & herbal remedies etc but nothing has worked.

More water. More fibre, more fruit and laxatives. You name it I tried it.

Been so bunged up, had the prune juice and laxatives daily.

Tried all the supermarket and Chemists usual products.

I have tried so many products… they work at first then gradually stop doing anything.

Even having been to hospital to try and fix the issue.

Over the last few years I've spent £100's on different tablets for constipation and constant painful bloating.

For creative

Name the graveyard out loud.

Listing what failed her — water, fibre, prune juice, laxatives — earns instant belief. It proves you know her, and sets up the killer line: “worked when nothing else did.”

Stage 04 · The wall

So of course she doesn't believe you

The low point. She's not hopeful — she's braced for another let-down. "Sceptical" isn't an objection to overcome; it's her resting state, and it's her most-used word in the entire review set.

I'll be honest — I was deeply sceptical that this would work, after years of chronic constipation.

I was sceptical at first, thinking it would be another brand claiming things worked naturally.

I wasn't convinced it would do all it says.

I didn't have high hopes when I ordered, having tried several alternatives.

I was a bit dubious that these would live up to the strong reviews, and be another thing I bought via socials that wasn't very good.

For creative

Don't fight the scepticism — say it for her.

“I was sceptical at first, but…” is her exact arc. Opening on her doubt (a review-style, contrarian hook) disarms it faster than any claim. “Actually” is your highest-signal word — it means she didn't expect it to work.

Stage 04½ · The fear underneath

And she's scared of getting hooked on the fix

Even wanting relief, she's wary of trading one trap for another. Dependency is a live fear — trigger it and you lose her; reassure it and you win.

I worry that I will become reliant. That's not great.

I didn't want to be on laxatives forever.

They work at first then gradually stop — will this one too?

For creative

Frame it as daily support, never a crutch.

Lean on gentle, natural, keeps me regular — the language of a habit, not a rescue. Avoid anything that sounds like a laxative she'll be chained to.

The unlock. Speed plus gentleness. It moves in days, sometimes hours, without the cramping and pain the laxatives gave her. Surprise is the dominant emotion here.

Amazing product that worked when nothing else did.

The stuff works without tying your guts in knots, which has been my experience with laxatives.

No pain either, so definitely not a laxative — I'm very sensitive to anything harsh.
